Executive Summary Tradevance is designed as an AI-native global trade platform that combines discovery, intelligence, trust, matching, controlled introductions and end-to-end trade workflow into one operating layer. The strategy deliberately goes beyond a conventional B2B marketplace by separating a broad public discovery experience from a protected transactional Trade OS. Strategic Positioning Tradevance is not “another Alibaba”. The intended category is a Global Trade Operating System: a governed infrastructure layer that helps companies discover counterparties, qualify evidence, form opportunities, execute commercial workflows and maintain an auditable transaction context. Core strategic thesis Lower discovery friction: visitors can explore meaningful public trade intelligence before creating an account. Raise transaction trust: sensitive actions require authentication, role controls, verification and transaction-risk gates. Use AI as an orchestration layer: AI parses demand, qualifies supply, recommends matches, normalizes quotes, surfaces risks and prepares next actions, while humans retain approval of material actions. Protect commercial relationships: introductions, private opportunity data and transaction records are scope-controlled. Build a compounding data moat: each verified company, product, demand, quote, decision and execution event can improve matching, trust and intelligence — subject to consent, policy and lawful data use. What success looks like Tradevance becomes a trusted global destination for discovering and qualifying B2B trade counterparties. Buyers reduce time-to-qualified-supply and execution friction. Sellers increase qualified demand exposure and reduce low-quality inquiries. Operators gain an auditable command layer for risk, trade intelligence and transaction orchestration. Tradevance monetizes recurring software access plus transaction and managed-service economics where applicable. 2. Market Problem &amp; Structural Gap The problem Global B2B sourcing remains fragmented across directories, brokers, messaging, spreadsheets, trade documents, payment providers and logistics workflows. A buyer can find a supplier without knowing whether the supplier is credible, relevant, available, financially credible or suitable for the exact transaction. A supplier receives inquiries without enough context to know whether a buyer is qualified, genuine or ready to transact. Commercial decisions often require manual normalization of price, freight, insurance, Incoterms, payment terms, quality and risk. Trust data and transaction data often sit in separate systems, making it harder to form a complete counterparty view. The category gap Tradevance is designed to fill Traditional model Persistent gap Tradevance response Directory / marketplace Discovery without full transaction governance Public discovery + qualification + protected transaction workflow Broker-heavy sourcing Relationship-led but difficult to scale and audit Software-orchestrated matching and controlled introduction Data intelligence tool Signals without transaction execution Signals tied directly to sourcing, quotes and execution ERP / TMS / trade finance tool Execution systems without global counterparty discovery Upstream discovery + qualification + downstream workflow AI chat interface Advice without system-of-record controls AI embedded inside governed workflow and permission boundaries 5. AI Intelligence &amp; Agent Architecture AI design principle AI should be deeply embedded but tightly bounded. It must make the platform faster and smarter without silently becoming the principal decision-maker for high-impact commercial actions. Core AI capabilities AI Demand Intake — convert natural-language demand into structured requirements. Matching Engine — identify supply candidates using product, geography, quantity, timing, commercial and trust factors. Opportunity Engine — rank opportunities using demand strength, supply fit, trust, commercial fit and risk. Quote Intelligence — normalize and compare quotes. Decision Mesh — surface trade-offs and next-best actions. Risk Intelligence — combine evidence, sanctions signals, transaction context and exceptions. Tradevance Copilot — natural-language operating assistant over authorized platform context. Agent Orchestration — Procurement Agent, Compliance Agent, Logistics Agent, Finance Agent and Negotiation support, bounded by role and approval gates. AI governance AI outputs must distinguish evidence from inference. AI cannot approve material transactions merely because it recommends them. System actions that change money, legal position, counterparties or transaction state require explicit authorization. Model/provider interfaces should remain replaceable. Prompt/output confidentiality and customer data boundaries must be enforced. AI claims must remain aligned with actual capabilities; the legal framework explicitly rejects absolute claims such as “100% verified” or “guaranteed safe” without evidence. Competitive Positioning &amp; Moat Positioning Tradevance should not compete head-on by being “a bigger catalog”. It should compete by being the system that makes the catalog useful: qualified identity, contextual intelligence, controlled introductions and transaction workflow. Moat development Network density — more qualified buyers + sellers improves matching. Trust graph — accumulated evidence, verification and transaction history improves qualification. Workflow graph — more RFQs, quotes and decisions create better commercial normalization. Intelligence graph — structured entity/product/opportunity relationships create increasingly useful AI context. Human-in-the-loop feedback — approved outcomes improve model and rule quality. Enterprise integrations — deeper workflow embedding raises switching costs. 18. Network Effects &amp; Flywheel Core flywheel More buyers → more supplier opportunity → more sellers → better supply coverage → more successful matches → more transaction evidence → stronger trust signals → better intelligence → more buyers. Liquidity strategy Start geographically and vertically focused enough to create real liquidity. Use curated demand and supply seeding rather than waiting for organic marketplace dynamics. Prioritize categories where quality/qualification is valuable and repeat trade is plausible. Track not just sign-ups but qualified counterparties, qualified opportunities, RFQ response rate, quote quality and completion rate. 19. Financial Strategy &amp; Economic Logic Financial logic The financial objective is to create recurring software revenue while using transaction and managed-service economics to capture value from high-intent commercial activity. The business should avoid becoming structurally dependent on low-margin brokerage work; managed services are a bridge to liquidity, trust and enterprise adoption. Major cost pools Engineering and product AI/model usage Data licensing Cloud infrastructure Compliance/KYB/sanctions providers Payment provider fees Sales/customer success Trade operations Legal and security Financial controls Recognize payment-linked revenue only from settled/verified processor state. Track provider costs per transaction or per intelligence request. Separate platform gross margin from managed-service gross margin. Measure CAC/payback by customer type and acquisition channel. 22. Decision Principles &amp; Non-Negotiables No public claim may exceed the underlying evidence or actual connected capability. No anonymous user may mutate protected commercial state. No role may access another party’s private data unless explicitly authorized. No AI recommendation may silently approve a material commercial action. No provider is described as live until its production integration is operational. No legal document is presented as effective before publication control is complete. No production package is labelled “byte-for-byte” unless it is actually exported from the source system. Every release must have a rollback path and a documented QA status. 26. Current Status &amp; External Dependencies Current product status The current platform baseline in AppDeploy includes the public discovery concept, authenticated Trade OS, buyer/seller role separation, legal center, verification ladder, AI-assisted trade workflows, payment state-machine boundary and multiple authorization/data-isolation hardenings. The running application has been maintained with a production-conservative approach: when a requested patch could not be safely merged, the last known healthy baseline was preserved. Known external dependencies Xendit business verification and production integration are still external to the codebase. Legal documents require effective-date/publication control before being represented as operative terms; the master legal pack explicitly calls for this. [Source: Tradevance Legal &amp; Compliance Master Pack, publication checklist] Formal Tradevance trademark/brand clearance remains a global-launch gate. tradevance.com DNS activation remains a launch task. Live trade/shipment/KYB/sanctions datasets require the relevant licensed/provider connections. Current AppDeploy project surface has not provided automated E2E runs for every browser path; final manual E2E remains a launch gate. Management conclusion Tradevance should now be managed as a launch-candidate platform, not as a perpetual prototype. The product priority is liquidity, trust, data connectivity, transaction reliability and enterprise adoption — not endless feature accumulation. Appendix A.
